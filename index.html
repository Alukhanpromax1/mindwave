      How it works · the proposed system

      From imagination to visual feedback

      Six proposed steps. Select any step to read what a real system would do — and what this web prototype actually does instead.

        Imagining is not automatically readable

        Imagined movement does not automatically produce a reliably recognisable EEG pattern. Interpreting a signal would require calibration, careful testing and adaptation to each individual — and even then, a classifier can be uncertain or wrong.

        Around the pipeline — not steps inside it

        Calibration

        Before any use, a supervised calibration would record example signals so the system could learn which patterns are relevant for this particular person. It would be repeated and adjusted over time.

        Professional oversight

        Qualified rehabilitation professionals would guide task selection, set-up, session pacing and interpretation. The system would support their work, never replace it.

        In the full concept, the movement task would be chosen with the child, family and rehabilitation professionals.

        Personal set-up · a conceptual story

        Every person is different.

        Three gentle steps show how a future system might be set up around one person. Nothing here records or calibrates anything.

        Professional oversight checkpoint

        Calibration and task selection would be supervised by appropriately qualified professionals.

        A real calibration would need actual EEG recordings, technical checks, ethical oversight and a professional who knows the child. This prototype contains none of those — it only tells the story.

        D

        Individual differences and motor imagery

        Published research · Fierro-Marrero et al., 2024

        A 2024 systematic review of motor-imagery ability in children and adolescents with cerebral palsy included seven cross-sectional studies (174 participants with CP and 321 typically developing participants). It found that evidence remained limited for drawing clear conclusions about several explicit motor-imagery abilities and about motor-imagery / execution synchrony.

        Existing evidence does not justify assuming that all children have the same ability to generate or perform imagined movements. Children with CP are not one uniform group: abilities, experiences, sensory needs and preferences differ.

        This is why MindWave explores individualised task selection rather than a single universal task.

        E

        Comfort and fatigue

        Published research · Keough et al., 2024 · Huggins et al., 2022

        Pediatric BCI research has identified variation in comfort, fatigue and individual experience. Keough and colleagues studied fatigue during BCI sessions in 32 children aged 7–16. Self-reported fatigue and EEG alpha-band power increased across sessions, and headset tolerability, motivation and individual experiences varied. The authors noted that implications for usability and enjoyment remained uncertain.

        Important: that study was not a clinical study of children with cerebral palsy. We use it only to motivate flexible session lengths, user-controlled breaks and a calm experience — it does not establish a clinical session schedule for CP rehabilitation.

        Huggins, Karlsson and Warschausky (2022) discussed challenges of BCI-facilitated cognitive assessment for children with CP, including accessibility, participation, EEG headgear fit, set-up, comfort and individual needs.

        G

        Current limitations and future work

        • No actual EEG hardware is connected.
        • The current application uses simulated input.
        • No live EEG signal is collected.
        • The current game has no validated BCI classifier.
        • The preliminary offline result is limited (≈57% vs a 50% chance-level reference, 13 adult participants).
        • The system has not been tested for clinical effectiveness.
        • Further research, expert collaboration, technical validation, accessibility assessment and appropriate ethical oversight would be required.
        MindWave is a research concept, not a finished medical technology. Our next step is to understand the problem more deeply before claiming a solution.
